# Biofuels desk — style

## The lede

The number and its direction first: "Ethanol production fell to 1,000 thousand barrels a day last week, the lowest
since …" Then the driver. The reader is a market professional who already knows what a RIN is; they want the move, the
why, and the next thing to watch.

## Length and shape

- **briefing** — 300–500 words; the move, the why, what to watch.
- **data-release** — 400–600 words, built around the released numbers in a table or chart where they warrant one.
- **market-wrap** — 400–600 words; lead with the biggest move of the session.
- **explainer** — 600–1,000 words; mechanism first, then the current stakes.
- **news-short** — 150–250 words; one development, tightly.
- **research** — as long as the question needs; every section carries its own sources.

## Voice

Flat, specific, no boosterism. Prices and volumes are facts with a source, not adjectives. No "plunged" when it fell
1%; no "surged" when it rose 2%. Watch words: "reportedly", "sources say", "expected" all need a named source or they
go.

## Numbers

- Every price, level, percentage, and volume carries its source URL inline.
- State the unit on first use and the change versus the prior period where you have it.
- RIN prices in cents/RIN; LCFS credits in $/metric ton; ethanol production in thousand barrels/day; stocks in
  million barrels.

## The reader already knows

What a RIN is, what an RVO is, what LCFS stands for. Do not re-teach the glossary; do not spell out acronyms on
second use.

# Crude-oil desk — style

## The lede

The surprise against expectations first: "Cushing stocks fell 3 million barrels last week, against a build of
1 million expected …" Then the driver and the read-through to WTI. The reader already knows WTI settled at Cushing;
they want the balance and the direction.

## Length and shape

- **briefing** — 300–500 words; the move, the why, what to watch.
- **data-release** — 400–600 words, built around the WPSR tables, with a chart for Cushing, production, or runs when
  the series earns one.
- **market-wrap** — 400–600 words; lead with the biggest move of the session.
- **explainer** — 600–1,000 words; mechanism first (how a Cushing draw moves WTI), then the current stakes.
- **news-short** — 150–250 words; one development, tightly.
- **research** — as long as the question needs; every section carries its own sources.

## Voice

Flat, specific, no boosterism. Draws and builds are numbers with a source, not drama. No "plunged" when it fell 1%.
Watch words: "reportedly", "sources say", "expected" all need a named source or they go.

## Numbers

- Every level, change, and percentage carries its source URL inline.
- Stocks in million barrels; production, runs, and imports in thousand barrels/day (kb/d); prices in $/bbl.
- Always state the change versus the prior week and versus the same week a year ago where the WPSR gives you both.

## The reader already knows

What Cushing is, what WTI is, what the WPSR is. Do not re-teach the glossary; do not spell out acronyms on second
use.
